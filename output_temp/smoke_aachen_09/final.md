# Question
What does Aachen do for rooftop solar?

## Executive Summary

Aachen’s rooftop-solar strategy is strikingly policy-rich but target-light: the evidence shows future PV requirements for new buildings, immediate municipal-building and major-roof-renovation requirements, municipal-roof prioritisation, a solar-roof register, and owner support, but no grounded numeric rooftop-capacity target for either municipal or private roofs.[ref_1][ref_2][ref_3][ref_8][ref_11][ref_12]

A safe rooftop-capacity total cannot be stated; the only grounded scale numbers are almost 1,600 subsidised solar installations by May 2023 and €1.6 million of funding volume in 2023 alone, both for Aachen, while numeric target coverage is 0/1 for municipal roofs and 0/1 for private roofs.[ref_9]

This answer uses 13 CCC excerpts plus one governed external action-plan table entry, with no web-sourced updates and no estimates because both rooftop-capacity-target gaps remained non-estimable.

## CCC Document Key Findings

- **Requirements and standards:** Aachen says photovoltaic systems will be mandatory for new buildings in future, seeks to combine rooftop PV with green roofs where possible, and aims to use all suitable roof surfaces for solar.[ref_1] PV systems are also to be installed with immediate effect on all new municipal buildings and in major renovations, especially roof refurbishments.[ref_12]
- **Municipal estate and public assets:** The city has already identified and prioritised suitable roof areas on municipal properties for PV expansion to increase self-generated renewable electricity.[ref_2][ref_5] Aachen also wants its electricity to come entirely from renewable sources, ideally including power from the city’s own rooftop photovoltaic systems.[ref_6] When refurbishing municipal housing stock, the city also examines installing PV systems.[ref_13]
- **Support, funding, and activation:** Aachen supports building owners with advisory services to help them realise PV systems on roof surfaces.[ref_3][ref_7] It complements this with funding and an activation campaign to expand rooftop PV.[ref_8] By May 2023, almost 1,600 solar installations had been subsidised since the programme began in September 2020, with €1.6 million in funding volume in 2023 alone.[ref_9]
- **Planning tools and constraints:** Aachen treats rooftop PV as part of sustainable construction and more energy-positive buildings.[ref_4] The city has also created a solar roof register and identified large solar potential.[ref_11] In the historic centre of Kornelimünster, rooftop PV is subject to heritage approval requirements.[ref_10]

## Cross-City Thematic Synthesis

With only Aachen selected, this section compares policy instruments rather than cities.

### Policy architecture

Aachen layers several rooftop-solar levers rather than relying on a single programme: a future PV requirement for new buildings, immediate PV installation requirements for new municipal buildings and major roof refurbishments, municipal-roof prioritisation, and owner-facing support through advice, funding, and activation.[ref_1][ref_2][ref_3][ref_8][ref_12]

This creates a broad delivery model across both public and private building stock. On the municipal side, Aachen has identified roof space for rollout and links that rollout to self-generation for city electricity use.[ref_2][ref_5][ref_6] On the building-renovation side, it also considers PV during refurbishment of municipal housing stock.[ref_13]

### Scope and quantification

Coverage is asymmetric. Qualitative rooftop-solar policy evidence exists for 1/1 selected city, but numeric rooftop-capacity-target evidence exists for 0/1 municipal roofs plus 0/1 private roofs = 0/2 scope-specific target values.

The bundle gives one programme-scale output metric: almost 1,600 subsidised solar installations by May 2023, with €1.6 million in funding volume in 2023 alone.[ref_9] These are distinct units, so there is no meaningful arithmetic rooftop-solar total such as `1,600 + €1.6 million`.

A governed external action-plan table does identify a municipal measure called `PV-Anlagen auf kommunalen Gebäuden`, but the record remains review-required and non-numeric, so it is excluded from any capacity sum or target subtotal (klimastadtvertrag-action-plan:L1462-L1482). For private rooftops, searched external material found qualitative rooftop-PV actions but no concrete MWp/kWp/GWp target, so the citywide/private ambition remains qualitative in this evidence set.

### Delivery tools and implementation nuance

Aachen backs deployment with a solar roof register, advisory services, funding, and an activation campaign, suggesting the city is trying to convert rooftop potential into projects rather than relying only on formal obligations.[ref_3][ref_8][ref_11]

The city also frames rooftop PV as part of broader sustainable-construction practice, including combination with green roofs where possible.[ref_1][ref_4] The main implementation constraint visible in the evidence is heritage approval in protected areas such as Kornelimünster.[ref_10]
